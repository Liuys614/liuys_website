Sourdough Bulk Fermentation Chart: Temp vs. Time & Rise %

Published 2026-10-28 · 6 min read · Levain Artisanal Baking Series
Quick Answer — Sourdough Bulk Fermentation Chart & Proofing Time FormulaAt 80°F (27°C), shape at a 30–40% rise (4–5 hrs); at 75–76°F (24°C), shape at a 40–50% rise (5–6.5 hrs); and at 68°F (20°C), shape at a 75–100% rise (9–12 hrs) with 20% starter. Warmer dough needs a lower rise percentage before cold retard because it keeps fermenting for 3+ hours while cooling in the fridge. Use our bulk fermentation calculator below for custom temperatures and levain percentages.

Bulk fermentation—the first rise from mixing active levain until final shaping—sets most of your sourdough loaf's volume, crumb structure, and oven spring (alongside your sourdough hydration percentage and starter feeding ratio).

Many common sourdough failures (gummy bottoms or overproofed flat dough) trace back to following static recipes like "rise for 4 to 6 hours" while ignoring the primary driver: internal dough temperature.

Why Warmer Dough Needs a Lower Percentage Rise

As dough temperature climbs from 68°F to 80°F, wild yeast and lactic acid bacteria rates roughly double. When you transfer an 80°F (27°C) shaped loaf into a 38°F (3°C) refrigerator for an overnight cold retard, the core takes 3 to 4 hours to cool below 45°F (7°C)—continuing to ferment rapidly the entire time.

Conversely, 68°F (20°C) dough enters the refrigerator already cool and reaches yeast dormancy in under an hour. Shaping it at only a 30% rise would produce an underproofed, dense loaf.

Dough Temp vs. Room TempAlways measure internal dough core temperature with a probe thermometer rather than relying solely on kitchen air temperature. Mixing water temperature, starter warmth, and friction determine your real fermentation speed.

4 Sensory Cues + Aliquot Jar Check

Pair your sourdough fermentation temp chart with these four physical indicators before pre-shaping:

  1. Domed Perimeter: Properly fermented dough pulls away from the bowl edge in a gentle convex dome.
  2. The Jiggle Test: Gently shake the vessel; ripe dough wobbles like firm gelatin from suspended CO₂ cells.
  3. Sub-Surface Blisters: Translucent fermentation bubbles appear right beneath the top dough skin.
  4. Aliquot Jar Marker: Pinch 35g of mixed dough into a straight-sided spice jar with a rubber band at starting height so 30% vs. 50% rise is unmistakable.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you calculate sourdough bulk fermentation time?

To calculate sourdough bulk fermentation time, measure your dough's internal core temperature and account for starter inoculation percentage. For a standard 20% levain recipe: expect 4–5 hours at 80°F (30–40% rise), 5–6.5 hours at 76°F (40–50% rise), and 9–12 hours at 68°F (75–100% rise). Time starts when levain is mixed in and ends at shaping.

Is there a bulk fermentation calculator for sourdough to predict proofing time?

Yes. Use the interactive sourdough bulk fermentation calculator above by entering your internal dough temperature (°F) and starter inoculation (%). Because yeast fermentation speed roughly doubles every 10°F (5.5°C) increase and slows when you use less starter (halving inoculation adds roughly a third to the time in this model), temperature-adjusted timing prevents dense underproofed loaves and overproofed pancake dough.

How do I read a sourdough bf temp chart to prevent overproofing?

A sourdough fermentation temp chart (or bf temp chart) pairs internal dough temperature with the target volume rise percentage before cold retarding. In warm kitchens (80°F–84°F / 27°C–29°C), stop bulk early at 20%–40% rise so carryover heat in the fridge does not overproof the gluten matrix.

What happens if you over-bulk ferment sourdough?

Over-fermented dough accumulates excess organic acids that weaken gluten bonds. The dough turns sticky and slack, refuses to hold surface tension during shaping, and bakes flat with poor oven spring.

Can I bulk ferment sourdough overnight on the counter?

Only if your kitchen stays cool (60°F–65°F / 15°C–18°C) and you lower starter inoculation to 5%–10%. At standard room temperatures (70°F+) with 20% starter, an overnight counter bulk fermentation will severely overproof the dough.

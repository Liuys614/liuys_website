Research notes

Cravings rise and pass.
Recovery takes time.

01 · Urge surfing

An urge rises, peaks, and passes if you wait it out

  1. RiseA cue such as 5 PM, stress, or a routine habit.
  2. PeakNotice the urge without acting on it.
  3. PassMany people find cravings ease within minutes as you breathe and wait.

Urge-surfing studied in Bowen & Marlatt (2009), Psychology of Addictive Behaviors; urge coping tips in NIAAA Rethinking Drinking.

02 · Try one round

Paced breathing gives you a minute to pause

Ready 4 · 6

4-second inhale and 6-second exhale timer to help you wait out cravings (NIAAA Rethinking Drinking).

03 · What the app tracks

Days, check-ins, and money saved

  • 0daily check-in levels, from alcohol-free to heavy
  • 0breathing rounds per one-minute pause
  • 0%of your logs stored locally on your device

CalmDry counts days, drinks, and estimated savings locally. It does not measure biological markers.

Quick answers

Frequently asked questions

Where is my tracker data stored?

Your profile, check-ins, drink levels, times, and notes are stored locally on your device. The app does not upload your personal logs to any external server.

Do I need an account?

No. Alcohol Free Tracker works immediately without registration, login, or an internet connection.

Can I track moderation ("drink less") as well as complete sobriety?

Yes. Daily check-ins let you log an alcohol-free day or record a light, moderate, or heavy drink level without resetting your logged reflection streak.

How do I back up or transfer my data?

Open Settings, choose Export, and save the portable JSON file to your files or iCloud Drive. To restore it on a new phone, choose Import.

Is this medical treatment?

No. The app is a self-directed tracking tool, not medical advice, diagnosis, treatment, counseling, or emergency withdrawal support.

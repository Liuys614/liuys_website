When to Take Creatine: Before or After Workout?

Published 2026-10-28 · 5 min read · Supplement & Medication Series
Quick Answer: Best Time to Take Creatine: Some lifters prefer taking 3–5 g of creatine after a workout with a meal or shake, while others take it before training. According to the International Society of Sports Nutrition position stand (Kreider et al., 2017), a maintenance dose of about 3–5 g per day is safe and well tolerated in healthy people, and taking creatine consistently every day (including rest days) matters more than exact workout timing.

Creatine monohydrate is one of the most widely used sports supplements for supporting strength and high-intensity exercise performance, and Kreider et al. (2017) note that it is generally well tolerated in healthy adults. Yet among gym-goers, one debate keeps coming up: Is it better to take creatine before your workout or immediately after?

While marketing often promotes complex pre-workout blends, creatine works by gradually building up muscle stores over weeks rather than providing an immediate stimulant effect. Here is what current guidance says about timing, taking it with food, and building a steady daily routine.

Pre-Workout vs. Post-Workout Timing

People often debate whether taking creatine right before or right after lifting makes a meaningful difference. Some researchers have explored whether increased blood flow and post-exercise meals make muscles slightly more receptive right after training, while taking creatine beforehand is equally convenient for many lifters.

Overall, evidence comparing exact pre- vs. post-workout timing is limited, and any difference between the two windows appears small. The ISSN position stand (Kreider et al., 2017) emphasizes that taking a consistent maintenance dose of roughly 3–5 g per day matters far more than the exact minute you take it.

The Golden Rule: Consistency Beats Exact Timing: Even if post-workout timing offers a small edge for some people, taking creatine daily at any convenient hour is much more important than taking it at the "perfect" time only 4 days a week. Muscle creatine stores build up gradually over several weeks of steady 3–5 g daily dosing, and skipping days slows that process down.

3 Practical Tips for Taking Creatine

  1. Consider Pairing with a Meal or Shake: Taking creatine alongside a meal containing carbohydrates and protein may help support uptake and is often gentler on the stomach than taking it on an empty stomach.
  2. Stay Well Hydrated: Creatine draws water into muscle tissue. Drinking plenty of water throughout the day helps keep you hydrated and may reduce mild stomach discomfort.
  3. Stick to Plain Creatine Monohydrate: Standard creatine monohydrate is the best-studied form and is well supported for safety and efficacy (Kreider et al., 2017). More expensive variants have not shown clear advantages over regular monohydrate.

Why Routine Reminders Matter for Daily Supplements

Because creatine does not give you an immediate sensation like caffeine does (which many people feel within minutes), it is easy to forget your daily scoop on non-training days. Pairing a daily reminder with morning hydration or your evening meal helps keep your routine consistent year-round.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it better to take creatine before or after working out?

Research comparing pre- and post-workout timing is limited, and differences appear small. Either before or after training works well for most people as long as you take a consistent daily dose of about 3–5 g.

When should you drink creatine on workout days vs rest days?

On workout days, you can mix creatine into a post-training shake or take it with a meal before or after training. On rest days, drink it with breakfast or another regular meal at whatever time is easiest to remember.

Do I take creatine before or after a workout if I train fasted?

If you train fasted, many people prefer taking creatine after their workout with their first meal. Taking creatine on an empty stomach can cause mild gastrointestinal discomfort in some individuals, so pairing it with food is often easier to tolerate.

Do I need to take creatine on rest days?

Yes. Creatine works by keeping muscle stores topped up over time, so skipping rest days can gradually lower those levels. Take your regular 3–5 g maintenance dose on rest days with a meal or drink.

Do I need to do a loading phase with creatine?

A loading phase (often around 20 g daily split into smaller doses for several days) can build up muscle stores faster, though it may cause bloating or stomach upset for some people. Taking a steady 3–5 g daily reaches similar levels over several weeks and is usually gentler on the stomach.

Does caffeine cancel out the effects of creatine?

Some people experience mild stomach discomfort when combining large amounts of caffeine and creatine at the exact same time, though evidence on whether caffeine blunts creatine is limited. If it bothers your stomach, take caffeine pre-workout and creatine with a meal afterward.

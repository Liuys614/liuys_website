Agricola Pasture Fencing Layout: Max Animals with 15 Fences

Published 2026-11-06 · 6 min read · Agricola Companion Rules Series
Quick Answer — Optimal 15-Fence Agricola Layout: The highest-yield fence layout uses all 15 wooden fences to enclose a 2×3 grid (6 farm spaces) split into 4 separate pastures (two 2-space and two 1-space pastures). This reaches the maximum 4 Victory Points for pastures, holds up to 24 animals with 4 stables, and covers 6 farmyard spaces to avoid empty-tile penalties.

In Uwe Rosenberg's worker-placement classic Agricola, each player receives exactly 15 wooden fence pieces. Once placed, fences cannot be moved or reconfigured. Because unused farmyard spaces inflict a -1 point penalty, and missing any animal type (sheep, wild boar, cattle) deducts -1 point each, optimal fence layout geometry is vital for competitive play.

Understanding pasture thresholds alongside overall Agricola scoring rules transforms fences from a wood drain into a high-scoring endgame engine.

Why the 15-Fence Limit Dictates Board Geometry

The borders of your 3×5 player board do not count as fences; every side must be enclosed by wooden fences. Building a single standalone pasture costs 4 fences and holds 2 animals. Building two separate single pastures costs 8 fences and holds 4 animals.

To maximize efficiency, competitive players build contiguous pastures that share internal borders. Because pasture points cap at 4 pastures (1 pasture = 1 VP, 2 = 2 VP, 3 = 3 VP, 4+ = 4 VP), any layout yielding fewer than 4 pastures forfeits free endgame victory points.

The Blueprint: 2×3 Four-Pasture Master Layout

The standard tournament setup encloses a 2-row by 3-column rectangle on your farm grid:

  • Outer Perimeter (10 Fences): 3 top, 3 bottom, 2 left, 2 right = 10 fences.
  • Internal Dividers (5 Fences): 2 vertical between columns 1 and 2; 2 vertical between columns 2 and 3; and 1 horizontal dividing column 2 = 5 fences.

This layout uses all 15 fences to create four dedicated pastures:

  1. Pasture 1 (2 spaces): Holds 4 animals (8 with 1 stable). Best for Sheep (needs 8+ for max 4 VP).
  2. Pasture 2 (1 space): Holds 2 animals (4 with 1 stable). Ideal for Wild Boar (needs 7+ for 4 VP, or 3–4 for 2 VP).
  3. Pasture 3 (1 space): Holds 2 animals (4 with 1 stable). Ideal for Cattle (needs 6+ for 4 VP, or 2–3 for 2 VP).
  4. Pasture 4 (2 spaces): Holds 4 animals (8 with 1 stable). Flexible overflow for breeding or harvest slaughter in your food engine.

Base capacity is 12 animals. With 4 stables (one per pasture), capacity reaches 24 animals. Covering 6 tiles also eliminates 6 potential unused space penalties (-6 VP).

Stable Capacity Math & Doubling Rules

Stables cost 2 wood each. Their interaction with fences determines livestock capacity:

  • Unfenced Stable: Holds 1 animal of any kind on an open farmyard tile and eliminates the -1 unused space penalty.
  • Fenced Stable: A stable inside an enclosed pasture doubles that pasture's capacity (a 1-space pasture holds 4; a 2-space pasture holds 8).
  • Multiple Stables: Each additional stable doubles capacity again (e.g., a 2-space pasture with 2 stables holds 16 animals). However, placing 1 stable in each of your 4 pastures is superior for species separation.
  • House Pet: Your farmhouse holds 1 pet animal for free, regardless of fences or family size.

Livestock Scoring & Breeding Mechanics

Maxing out animal points requires 8 sheep (4 VP), 7 boar (4 VP), and 6 cattle (4 VP), for 12 VP. Combined with 4 VP for 4 pastures, livestock delivers up to 16 victory points.

During Harvest, having at least 2 animals of a species produces 1 newborn. Offspring require open pasture or stable space; if pastures are full, the newborn is discarded unless converted into food immediately via a Fireplace or Cooking Hearth.

4 Strategic Fencing Rules

  1. Never Fence Before Stage 3: Prioritize wood for rooms (5 wood + 2 reed) and family growth in Stages 1–2.
  2. Fence in a Single Action: The Fences action lets you place up to 15 fences at once. Save wood and build your entire grid in one turn.
  3. Protect 5 Spaces for Plowed Fields: Place your 2×3 pasture block in an outer corner so the remaining 9 tiles can fit your farmhouse and 5 plowed fields (4 VP).
  4. Use Unfenced Stables Early: If 3+ sheep accumulate in Stage 2, build an unfenced stable or house a pet to secure the breeding pair before committing to fences.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many animals can you hold in Agricola with 15 fences?

With 15 fences, base capacity is 12 animals in a 2×3 four-pasture grid (or 16 in a 2×4 three-pasture grid). With 4 stables in the 2×3 grid, capacity doubles to 24 animals. Adding 1 house pet brings the farm total to 25 animals.

Does a stable double pasture capacity or add 2 spaces in Agricola?

A stable inside a pasture doubles that pasture's total capacity. A 1-space pasture holds 2 animals normally, or 4 with a stable. A 2-space pasture holds 4 animals normally, or 8 with a stable. An unfenced stable outside holds only 1 animal.

Can different animal types share the same pasture in Agricola?

No. Under official Agricola rules, each pasture can only contain one species (sheep only, boar only, or cattle only). You need at least 3 separate pastures to hold all three animal types simultaneously.

Can you move or remove fences once built in Agricola?

No. Fences are permanent once built and cannot be moved or removed. Furthermore, all fences placed during an action must immediately complete fully enclosed pasture boundaries.

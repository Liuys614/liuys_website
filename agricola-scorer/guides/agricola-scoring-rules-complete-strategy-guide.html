Agricola Board Game Scoring Rules: The Complete Point Calculation Guide

Published 2026-10-27 · 6 min read · Agricola Companion Rules Series
Quick Answer — How Agricola scoring worksAfter round 14, score 7 farm categories (fields, pastures, grain, vegetables, sheep, wild boar, cattle) from -1 to 4 points each, for a maximum of 28. Then add 1 point per fenced stable, 1 per clay room, 2 per stone room, 3 per family member, and card points. Subtract 1 per unused farmyard space and 3 per begging card.

Uwe Rosenberg's Agricola ends after the harvest of round 14, and every player then scores their farm on the same chart. The arithmetic is simple, but there are a dozen line items, and the most common mistakes (forgetting fenced stables, missing crops still on fields, miscounting unused spaces) can swing a close game by 3 to 5 points.

The chart below is the base-game category chart. It rewards a balanced farm: a category you never touched costs you 1 point, and every category stops paying at 4 points no matter how far you push it.

Two things trip people up here. First, grain and vegetables count wherever they are: in your supply and still sitting on plowed fields. Second, animals count anywhere on your farm, including the one pet allowed in your house and any animals kept on cards.

An unfenced stable is worth 0 points, but it still fills its space, so it saves you the -1 penalty. That makes a lone stable on a leftover square a cheap 1-point swing late in the game.

Worked Example: Scoring a 40-Point Farm

Here is a typical mid-level farm at the end of round 14: 4 fields, 3 pastures holding 6 sheep, 3 boar and 2 cattle, 5 grain, 2 vegetables, 2 fenced stables, 4 clay rooms, 4 family members, 1 empty space, no begging cards, and a Clay Oven, Pottery and Fireplace.

  • Categories: fields 3 + pastures 3 + grain 2 + vegetables 2 + sheep 3 + boar 2 + cattle 2 = 17
  • Fenced stables: 2 × 1 = 2
  • Clay rooms: 4 × 1 = 4
  • Family: 4 × 3 = 12
  • Unused space: 1 × -1 = -1
  • Improvements: Clay Oven 2 + Pottery 2 + Fireplace 1 = 5, plus 1 Pottery bonus for 3 leftover clay = 6
  • Total: 17 + 2 + 4 + 12 - 1 + 6 = 40

Notice where the points come from: family members and the category chart make up more than two thirds of the total. That is why growing the family early and covering every category usually beats maxing out one animal type.

Five Scoring Mistakes to Check Before You Add Up

  1. Crops left on fields not counted toward grain or vegetables.
  2. Fenced stables forgotten (1 point each).
  3. Unused spaces counted wrong: rooms, fields, pastures and stables all fill a space.
  4. Points above the 4-point cap counted for an oversized herd.
  5. Card bonuses from occupations and minor improvements skipped.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do grain and vegetables planted on fields count at the end of Agricola?

Yes. Grain and vegetables count both in your supply and still on your plowed fields. A field with 3 grain on it adds 3 to your grain total.

How many points is a fenced stable worth in Agricola?

1 point per stable inside a fenced pasture, so up to 4 points. An unfenced stable scores 0, but it fills its farmyard space and avoids the -1 unused-space penalty.

What is the maximum score from the Agricola category chart?

28 points: 4 points each for fields, pastures, grain, vegetables, sheep, wild boar and cattle. Anything beyond the 4-point threshold in a category adds nothing.

Do animals in the house count for scoring?

Yes. The one pet you may keep in your house counts toward its animal category, as do animals held on cards.

Agricola Feeding Strategy: How to Avoid Begging Cards

Published 2026-10-28 · 7 min read · Agricola Companion Rules Series
Quick Answer — How much food Agricola harvests costThere are 6 harvests (after rounds 4, 7, 9, 11, 13 and 14). In multiplayer games each family member eats 2 food, and a newborn from that round eats 1. A family of 2 that never grows needs 24 food in total; growing to 4 early pushes that past 40. Every missing food is one Begging Card worth -3 points.

The harvest in Uwe Rosenberg's Agricola is where most farms lose their game. After rounds 4, 7, 9, 11, 13 and 14 you run three steps in order: the field phase (take 1 grain or vegetable from each sown field), the feeding phase (pay 2 food per family member, 1 food for a member born this round), then the breeding phase (each animal type with at least 2 animals gets 1 newborn if you have room for it).

If you cannot pay, you take one Begging Card per missing food, and each card costs 3 points at the end. Two cards (-6) usually cost more than a whole scoring category is worth, since a category tops out at 4 points (see the full Agricola scoring rules). The plan below shows how much food you actually need and which engine covers it.

Example: grow to 3 in round 5 and to 4 in round 8. You pay 4 (round 4), 6 (round 7), then 8 at each of rounds 9, 11, 13 and 14. That is 42 food over the game, against 24 if you never grow. The extra 18 food buys you about 15 extra actions and 6 points for the two family members, so growth is worth it only when the food is already planned.

Conversion Rates: Raw, Fireplace, Cooking Hearth and Ovens

Grain and vegetables can always be eaten raw for 1 food each. Animals can only become food through a cooking improvement. The cooking improvements let you convert at any time, including in the middle of the feeding phase, without spending an action. Baking bread needs an action that allows it (Grain Utilization, or the free bake you get when you build an oven).

Food Engine First, Then Family Growth

The most common early mistake is taking Family Growth in stage 2 before any repeatable food source exists. A third worker gives you 50% more actions, but it also adds 2 food to every harvest that follows. Before you grow, check that you have at least one of these:

  1. A cooking improvement plus a breeding pair. Two sheep, two boar or two cattle with room for a third produce 1 animal per harvest. Cook the newborn surplus, keep the pair.
  2. Grain fields plus an oven. One sown grain field holds 3 grain and gives 1 per harvest for 3 harvests. With a Clay Oven, each Grain Utilization bake turns 1 grain into 5 food, so one bake covers a family of 2 with 1 food to spare.
  3. Vegetables plus a Cooking Hearth. Each sown vegetable field gives 2 vegetables over two harvests, worth 3 food each when cooked.

3 Food Engine Plans That Hold Up

  • Fireplace and sheep (rounds 1–4). Take 2 clay early, build a Fireplace, and take the Sheep space when it has piled up 2–3 sheep. At 2 food per sheep, 2 sheep cover the first harvest for a family of 2. Later, upgrade to a Cooking Hearth by returning the Fireplace.
  • Clay Oven and grain (stage 1–2). Sow 2 grain fields (6 grain total) and build the Clay Oven, which comes with a free bake. Each bake is 5 food from 1 grain. Grain left over at the end still scores in the grain category.
  • Cooking Hearth and boar or cattle (stage 3 onward). Boar and cattle appear later in the round deck. A cattle cooked on a Hearth gives 4 food, the best animal-to-food rate in the base game. Build pastures before the animals arrive; the 15-fence pasture layouts show how many animals each layout holds.

Occupations and minor improvements can add steady food too, but which ones you see depends on your hand, so plan the engine around the major improvements everyone can buy.

When is a Begging Card worth taking?Almost never before the last harvest. In round 14, taking one Begging Card (-3) can be correct if the action you would otherwise spend on food scores 4 or more points, such as building a major improvement worth 4 or more points.

Solo Games Are Harder on Food

The numbers above are for multiplayer games. In the solo game you start with 0 food and the feeding rules are stricter, so food plans need to start in round 1. The Agricola solo rules and food table covers that variant.

Counting the Final Score

End-game scoring tallies fields, pastures, grain, vegetables, sheep, wild boar and cattle, then 1 point per fenced stable, room points, 3 per family member, card points, -1 per unused farmyard space and -3 per Begging Card. Frequently Asked Questions

How much food do you need to feed each family member in Agricola?

Each family member needs 2 food at every harvest (after rounds 4, 7, 9, 11, 13 and 14). A family member born in the same round needs only 1 food at that harvest. A family of 2 needs 4 food per harvest; a family of 5 needs 10.

What is the penalty for a Begging Card in Agricola?

You take one Begging Card for each food you cannot pay at a harvest, and each card is worth -3 points at the end of the game. Missing 2 food means 2 cards, or -6 points.

Can you eat grain and vegetables raw in Agricola?

Yes. Grain and vegetables can be turned into 1 food each at any time without an improvement. A Fireplace or Cooking Hearth makes vegetables worth 2 or 3 food, and baking grain with an oven is worth 4 or 5 food per grain.

When can you cook animals for food in Agricola?

With a Fireplace or Cooking Hearth you can convert animals to food at any time, including during the feeding phase of a harvest, without using an action. Without one of those improvements, animals cannot be turned into food.

Is it better to build a Fireplace or a Cooking Hearth first?

A Fireplace is cheaper (2 or 3 clay) and is enough for sheep, which give 2 food either way. A Cooking Hearth (4 or 5 clay, or return your Fireplace) pays off once you cook vegetables, boar or cattle, each of which gives 1 more food on the Hearth.

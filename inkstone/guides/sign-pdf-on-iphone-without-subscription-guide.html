Method 1: Apple Markup (Best for Self-Signing)

If you only need to sign a document yourself (such as a permission slip or insurance claim):

  1. Open the PDF in the Files app or from your email attachment.
  2. Tap the circular Markup icon (pen tip) in the upper-right corner.
  3. Tap the + button at the bottom toolbar and choose Signature.
  4. Draw your signature with your finger or choose an existing saved signature, then drag and resize it into place.

Method 2: Sending a Document for Someone Else to Sign

The limitation of Apple Markup is that it only works on your own device. When you need a client, tenant, or business partner to sign a contract, forcing them to print, sign, scan, or download an app introduces massive friction and delays.

The Browser Signing Link AdvantageModern mobile workflows allow you to prepare document fields (signature boxes, dates, initials) on your phone and generate an encrypted web link. The recipient simply opens the link in Safari, Chrome, or Edge on their phone or laptop, signs with their finger or trackpad, and both parties receive the sealed PDF immediately.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are electronic signatures made on an iPhone legally binding?

Yes. Under the US ESIGN Act and the Uniform Electronic Transactions Act (UETA), electronically signed contracts carry the same legal weight as traditional pen-and-paper signatures when both parties demonstrate intent to sign.

Do my clients need an iPhone to sign a link I send from Inkstone?

No. When you share a signing link, recipients can open and sign the document on any smartphone, tablet, or desktop browser (including Android and Windows PC) without downloading an app or creating an account.

Is it safe to sign documents on mobile devices?

Yes, provided the tool utilizes local on-device PDF processing or end-to-end encrypted transfer. Documents prepared locally never risk being crawled by public web indexing bots.
